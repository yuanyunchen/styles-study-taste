What wins a web-design vote

Most of a vote is decided by which model drew the page and whether it works. The rest is learnable and carries over to later months and newer models: real photography, a full page and a deliberate headline typeface win; dominant gradients and sparse white minimalism lose.

173,200 votes on prompts naming no style (about 50,000 tournaments × 5 votes, less edits, corrupt brackets and style-named prompts); 198,152 pages; 142 models; votes 2026-01-01 to 2026-09-29; labels deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017 (model version), second labeler claude-haiku-5.5 on ~6% of votes

Five core trends come first: how preference moved (raw, and once model strength is removed), what models supply, what users ask for, and what models supply when users name no style. Preferences stayed largely stable; supply moved with each model generation.

Core trend 1: preference, raw win rate

Some looks win more of their head-to-head votes than others, month after month: real photos stay well above half, sparse minimal pages stay below.

Line-end value = Jul–Sep mean; in brackets, the change from the Jan–Mar mean (green = up, red = down, grey = no clear change: q≥.05 or under 0.5 pp). Photo pages won 54–58% of their votes in every month (55.8% overall [55.3, 56.4]); minimal-clean pages won 45–48% (46.9% [46.2, 47.6]). Share of votes won by the side that has the feature, among votes where the two sides differ on it; 95% CIs in text (voter-cluster bootstrap). Raw rates still carry each month's model mix, which panel 2 removes.

Core trend 2: preference beyond model strength and quality

Model strength aside, real photos win more than expected every month and sparse minimal pages lose; the gap barely moves over nine months.

Line-end value = Jul–Sep mean; in brackets, the change from the Jan–Mar mean (green = up, red = down, grey = no clear change: q≥.05 or under 0.5 pp). Photos stay +2.6 to +5.8 pp above expectation in every month (+4.2 pp [3.6, 4.8] overall, every monthly CI above 0); minimal-clean stays −1.3 to −3.7 pp below (−2.1 [−2.7, −1.4]). Actual minus expected win rate (Bradley–Terry model strength plus quality); 95% CIs in text. Serif headline and cream background lose their edge after June at the same time, possibly the same phenomenon (serif often pairs with cream). Purple-gradient pages turn positive in Aug–Sep, but single-month CIs include 0 on 1.3k and 0.8k votes: direction undetermined.

Core trend 4: what prompts ask for

Users increasingly ask for specific looks, above all dark pages and editorial type.

Line-end value = Jul–Sep mean; in brackets, the change from the Jan–Mar mean (green = up, red = down, grey = no clear change: CI includes 0 or under 0.5 pp). Requests rose for dark pages (11% in Jan to 23% in Sep; quarter means +6.7 pp [4.1, 9.2]), serif or editorial type (+6.4 [5.8, 7.0]) and cream backgrounds (+3.9 [3.2, 4.6]); violet requests stayed low (+0.9 [0.1, 1.8]). Core trend 5: supply once user-specified styles are removed

The same shift appears when every user-requested style is removed, so it comes from the models' own defaults.

Line-end value = Jul–Sep mean; in brackets, the change from the Jan–Mar mean (green = up, red = down, grey = no clear change: CI includes 0 or under 0.5 pp). With user-specified styles removed, violet accents went from 52% in Jan to 27% in Sep (quarter means −16.1 pp [−18.0, −14.1], slope q<10⁻⁷⁷); a chained decomposition puts 98% of the Jan-to-Sep fall on new models replacing old ones. Default supply = pages whose prompt named no style, tone or colour, excluding edits and pasted code (88,334 pages); 95% CIs in text; full-page series correlate 0.78–1.00. Cream is the exception: about a third of its Jan-to-Sep rise happened within models. All pages understate the purple-gradient exit: quarter means −8.0 pp all pages vs −10.6 [−11.7, −9.6] default.

What decides a vote

Which model drew the page matters most; between pages from one provider, a page that works and looks finished beats a page that merely picks a good style.

Which model drew the page carries half of the predictable signal, 51% [47, 56]; between pages from one provider, render and quality outweigh style about 3 to 1. Shapley share of held-out log-loss gain, user-bootstrap 95% CI; 35,468 test votes (all), 4,365 (same provider). Only 59.6% of held-out votes are predictable at all (AUC 0.636). In September, past model strength is no better than chance (accuracy 50.9%) while page features still help.

What wins at equal quality

At the same quality, real imagery, a complete page skeleton and a deliberate headline font win; empty space and system fonts lose.

Real photos beat icons by +4.4 pp [3.7, 5.1] (q=2×10⁻³⁴); generous whitespace and a system-font headline each lose about 2 pp. Win-probability difference (pp) with user-clustered 95% CI; model, quality and render controlled; 137–142k votes on prompts naming no style; labels name the reference. BH over 113 tests; none of 8 pre-declared combinations interacts (smallest q=.063). Filled dot = grade A (second labeler agrees, CI excludes 0); hollow = grade B.

Beyond what the model's strength predicts

Photo pages beat what their model's strength predicts, sparse minimal pages fall short, and dark styles are about neutral.

Photo pages win 4.2 pp [3.6, 4.8] more than their models' strength predicts (q<10⁻⁴⁴); minimal-clean falls 2.1 pp short; dark styles are roughly neutral once quality is counted. Actual minus expected win rate, expected from Bradley–Terry model strength plus quality; voter-cluster bootstrap 95% CI; BH over 63 tests. In-sample BT and Design Arena Elo baselines agree within 0.4 pp on attributes. As a family, editorial and luxury beat expectation by +2.5 pp [1.5, 3.6], q<.001.

Data we wanted

  • What the voter saw: viewport, device, live page or screenshot.
  • A one-tap reason and separate works / complete / looks-good scores.

Scaling without humans

  • A rule stays only if it replicates in next month's holdout.
  • Lint, holdout gate and second labeler replace review.

Culture

  • 1 of 60 scene tests significant (food photos).
  • Language, IP and device are proxies, not national taste.

Limits

  • Quality labels agree at 0.48; no user-level profiles.
